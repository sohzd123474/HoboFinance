title: Where your loan repayments go
description: Early repayments are mostly interest. Here is how a 25-year loan splits, and what a shorter term saves.

On a standard loan you pay the same amount every month, but the split between interest and paying off the loan changes all the way through.

## A worked example

Borrow 300,000 at 4% a year over 25 years. The repayment is 1,583.51 a month.

Interest is charged each month on what you still owe. In the first month that is 300,000 × 4% ÷ 12 = 1,000. So of the first 1,583.51 you pay, 1,000 is interest and only 583.51 reduces the loan.

Each month you owe slightly less, so slightly less interest is charged and slightly more of the repayment goes to the loan itself. Progress is slow at first:

- After 5 years you have paid about 95,000 and still owe 261,314.
- You do not get the balance below 180,000 until year 14, more than halfway through.

Over the full 25 years you repay 475,053, of which 175,053 is interest.

## What the term changes

The same 300,000 at 4% over different terms:

| Term | Monthly repayment | Total interest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 15 years | 2,219.06 | 99,431 |
| 25 years | 1,583.51 | 175,053 |
| 30 years | 1,432.25 | 215,609 |

Stretching from 25 to 30 years lowers the repayment by about 151 a month and adds about 40,500 in interest. A longer term is cheaper each month and more expensive overall.

## Why extra repayments help most early on

Any extra amount you pay comes straight off the balance, which lowers the interest charged in every month after it. An extra payment in year 2 saves interest for 23 years; the same payment in year 20 saves it for 5. Check whether your lender charges a fee for early repayment first.
