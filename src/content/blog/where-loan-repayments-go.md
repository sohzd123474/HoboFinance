description: Early repayments are mostly interest. See how a 25-year loan splits, and what a shorter term saves.

Pay extra early in a loan, not late. You pay the same amount every month, but in the early years most of it is interest.

## A worked example

Borrow 300,000 at 4% a year over 25 years. The repayment is 1,583.51 a month.

1. Interest is charged each month on what you still owe. In the first month that is 300,000 × 4% ÷ 12 = 1,000.
2. So of the first 1,583.51, 1,000 is interest and 583.51 reduces the loan.
3. Next month you owe slightly less, so slightly less is interest and slightly more reduces the loan.

Progress is slow at first:

- **After 5 years:** you have paid about 95,000 and still owe 261,314.
- **Year 14:** the balance first drops below 180,000. That is more than halfway through.
- **Over 25 years:** you repay 475,053, of which 175,053 is interest.

## What the term changes

The same 300,000 at 4% over different terms:

| Term | Monthly repayment | Total interest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 15 years | 2,219.06 | 99,431 |
| 25 years | 1,583.51 | 175,053 |
| 30 years | 1,432.25 | 215,609 |

Going from 25 to 30 years lowers the repayment by about 151 a month and adds about 40,500 in interest. A longer term costs less each month and more overall.

## Why extra repayments help most early on

- **An extra payment reduces the balance at once.** That lowers the interest charged in every month after it.
- **Timing matters.** An extra payment in year 2 saves interest for 23 years. The same payment in year 20 saves it for 5.
- **Check the fee first.** Some lenders charge for early repayment.
