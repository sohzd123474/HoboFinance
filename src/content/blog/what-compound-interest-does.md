---
title: What compound interest does to small amounts
description: Why 200 a month becomes far more than the sum of the deposits, and why starting early beats saving more later.
pubDate: 2026-11-13
---

Compound interest means you earn growth on your past growth, not only on the money you put in. In the first few years the effect is hard to see. Over decades it does most of the work.

## The same deposit over four time spans

Take 200 a month, growing at 6% a year. The amounts below are in whatever currency you use; the pattern is the same in all of them.

| Years | You put in | Growth | Balance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10 | 24,000 | 8,776 | 32,776 |
| 20 | 48,000 | 44,408 | 92,408 |
| 30 | 72,000 | 128,903 | 200,903 |
| 40 | 96,000 | 302,298 | 398,298 |

After 10 years, growth is about a quarter of the balance. After 40 years it is about three quarters. Each extra decade adds the same 24,000 in deposits but a much larger amount of growth.

## Starting early beats saving more

Compare two savers at the same 6%:

- One saves 200 a month for 30 years and puts in 72,000.
- The other waits 10 years, then saves 400 a month for 20 years and puts in 96,000.

The first ends with 200,903. The second ends with 184,816, despite putting in 24,000 more. The ten years of waiting cost more than doubling the deposit could make up.

## What the numbers leave out

A steady 6% is an average for illustration. Real investments rise and fall from year to year, fees reduce the return, and inflation reduces what the final balance buys. Use a lower rate if you want a more cautious estimate.
