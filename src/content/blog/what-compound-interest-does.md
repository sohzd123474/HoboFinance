---
title: What compound interest does to small amounts
description: 200 a month for 40 years becomes 398,298 at 6%, of which only 96,000 is deposits. Starting early beats saving more later.
pubDate: 2026-11-13
---

Start saving now, even if the amount is small. Compound interest means you earn growth on past growth, not only on the money you put in.

## The same deposit over four time spans

Take 200 a month, growing at 6% a year. The pattern is the same in any currency.

| Years | You put in | Growth | Balance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10 | 24,000 | 8,776 | 32,776 |
| 20 | 48,000 | 44,408 | 92,408 |
| 30 | 72,000 | 128,903 | 200,903 |
| 40 | 96,000 | 302,298 | 398,298 |

- **After 10 years:** growth is about a quarter of the balance.
- **After 40 years:** growth is about three quarters of the balance.
- **Each extra decade:** the same 24,000 in deposits, and a larger amount of growth than the decade before.

## Starting early beats saving more

Compare two savers at the same 6%:

| Saver | Plan | Puts in | Ends with |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Starts now | 200 a month for 30 years | 72,000 | 200,903 |
| Waits 10 years | 400 a month for 20 years | 96,000 | 184,816 |

The saver who waits puts in 24,000 more and ends with less. Doubling the deposit does not make up for the ten years.

## What the numbers leave out

- **Ups and downs.** A steady 6% is an average for illustration. Real investments rise and fall from year to year.
- **Fees.** They reduce the return.
- **Inflation.** It reduces what the final balance buys.

For a more cautious estimate, use a lower rate.
